# AraPheno Study 27 in-sample GroupSHAP and interactions

**Note date:** 2026-10-10 21:27:08

The analysis covered five RF and five KAN fold models for 81 accessions with
phenotype Mean(TRL) C, a fixed 200-SNP panel selected using the full phenotype,
and three full-cohort kinship PCs. It explained fitting and held-out samples on
the same panel. This design does not independently validate the phenotype-informed
panel-selection step.

The runner is `scripts/run_study27_training_groupshap.py` and the verifier is
`scripts/history/verify_study27_training_groupshap.py`. The current archive supplies
the frozen 81-by-203 feature matrix in `data/processed/arapheno_study27/fixed_panel.npz`.
Numerical results are retained under `outputs/arapheno_study27/training_heldout/`.

Earlier fixed-panel fits had no retained checkpoints. Models were reconstructed
with their recorded settings, seeds and splits, then checked against saved
predictions before explanations were accepted. RF prediction error was at most
1.42e-14 against the matched RF rerun; KAN error was at most 3.81e-6 against its
saved predictions. KAN training traces, selected epochs and held-out explanation
summaries also matched, with maximum summary discrepancy 6.76e-7.

The interventional game had six players: five chromosomes and one group containing
the three PCs. It enumerated all 64 coalitions and used eight fitting-only
background donors, sampled with seed `9000 + fold`. Donors were shared across
models and evaluation roles. Signed local values and full Shapley interaction
indices, without a one-half factor, were computed for every evaluation row in
the phenotype's native units. Background-sampling uncertainty was not assessed.

RF fitted weights on 64–65 rows per fold. KAN fitted weights on 51–52 rows and
used 13 separate validation rows for checkpoint selection. KAN summaries therefore
distinguish fitting, validation, combined outer-training and held-out rows.
Training summaries include background donors.

All ten reconstructed checkpoints reproduced the required predictions and
coalition games. Validation checked input/output hashes, exhaustive grouping,
sample separation and complete out-of-fold coverage. Maximum efficiency errors
were 8.08e-7 for float32 KAN and 3.55e-15 for RF. The in-sample analysis adds
explanation diagnostics; it neither refines panel selection nor establishes
independent biological validation.

Recorded mean within-fold fitting-versus-held-out comparisons were:

| Model | Fitting R² | Held-out R² | Group rank rho with PCs | Chromosome rank rho | All-pair rho | Chromosome-pair rho |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| RF | 0.8120 | 0.5597 | 0.9200 | 0.9000 | 0.9471 | 0.9394 |
| KAN | 0.8041 | 0.5344 | 0.9429 | 0.9400 | 0.9257 | 0.9030 |

The all-pair calculation covers 15 pairs; the chromosome-only calculation covers
ten. KAN combined outer-training/held-out interaction correlations were 0.9393
and 0.9394, respectively, which differ from fitting-only comparisons.
Chr1 ranked first in every fitting and held-out fold for both models. RF aggregate
chromosome order was Chr1, Chr5, Chr3, Chr2, Chr4 in both roles; KAN switched the
relative positions of Chr5 and Chr3 between fitting and held-out summaries.
Large fitting chromosome-pair means included Chr1×Chr5 and Chr1×Chr3.
These measure model reliance, not causal loci. Folds share fitting observations
and are not independent replications; held-out results remain the primary evaluation.
